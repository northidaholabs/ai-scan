2 · What you receive

  • A heat map per wall, like the one above, as a PNG.
  • A table per wall (CSV): length, height, lean overall and for the worst metre-wide strip, flatness RMS and 95th percentile, the straight-line check against your straightedge and tolerance, how much of the face was seen.
  • A plan of the walls in metres (DXF) and one HTML report with everything.

3 · What it takes on site

  • Stations that see each face you want checked; the agent marks faces nobody has seen yet and proposes where to stand next.
  • Full-dome scans, registered as you go. Here: 19 stations around and inside the courtyard.
  • The analysis runs afterwards in about half a minute on a desktop for this site.
  • Your tolerance: the straightedge length and the allowed gap (here 3 m and 6 mm).

4 · How sure

  • Repeatable, not yet calibrated. The stations of this site agree with each other within 22 mm pair by pair, and the bench checks that planted 3 cm errors are caught. Nobody has yet held a level or a total station to these walls to compare.
  • Registration shows up as roughness. A wall's plane mixes every station that saw it, so a few millimetres of mismatch between stations add to the flatness numbers.
  • Profiled surfaces measure as their profile. Ribbed cladding or siding reads as its ribs; that's correct, but it isn't the flatness of the wall behind.
  • Tall walls are seen at an angle. The top of a 15 m facade is far from a scanner on the street; its points are sparser and noisier than at eye level.
